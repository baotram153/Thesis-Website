## Abstract

End-to-end driving policies map raw sensor inputs directly to future waypoints or control commands, yet state-of-the-art models rely on large backbones whose latency and memory footprint preclude real-time deployment. While imitation learning and knowledge distillation can produce lightweight policies, they suffer from severe closed-loop degradation caused by distribution shift and causal confusion. We address this limitation through a three-stage training framework that synergistically combines imitation pre-training, knowledge distillation, and reinforcement learning fine-tuning, preserving the sample efficiency of supervised learning while achieving on-policy robustness. Our principal technical contributions are two-fold. First, we design a dense reward function that jointly optimizes safety, efficiency, and smoothness through context-aware target-speed modulation based on hazard proximity and road curvature. Second, we introduce an auxiliary guidance loss that propagates terminal-event feedback backward to preceding transitions, enabling more effective credit assignment and exploration during RL fine-tuning. On the Bench2Drive220 benchmark, our 26.4M-parameter policy achieves a Driving Score of 62.49 at 49.86ms latency—delivering the best performance-efficiency trade-off among all compared methods, with every higher-scoring alternative being several times larger and slower.
